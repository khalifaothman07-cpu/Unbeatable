Privacy

What this site keeps, why it keeps it, and how to get rid of it. It is short because there genuinely isn’t much.

The short version

This site counts visits so I can see whether anyone is reading it. It sets no cookies at all. It runs no advertising, no third-party analytics, and no tracking that follows you anywhere else. Nothing here is sold or shared, because there is nobody to sell it to.

What gets stored on your device

Four things, all in your browser’s local storage rather than in cookies. Two of them exist only because you asked for them; one needs your permission, and does not exist until you give it.

WhatWhyNeeds permission?
kaz6.theme Remembers whether you chose light, dark, or follow-my-device, so the site doesn’t flash the wrong one on every page. No — it’s a setting you chose.
kaz6.session Keeps you signed in, if you signed in. Without it, “sign in” could not work at all. No — it’s the thing you asked for.
kaz6.visitor One random number, so that two visits from this browser can be recognised as the same person rather than two strangers. Yes. It is not created unless you agree.
kaz6.consent Remembers your answer to the bar at the bottom, so it stops asking. No — remembering a refusal is the point of it.

The games keep a little more — your board, your seat and your theme — so a reload doesn’t throw away a game in progress. That stays in your browser and never reaches the visit log. If you play an online table, the game state is shared with the other people at that table, which is what makes it an online table.

Checking…

You can change your mind here whenever you like, in either direction. Saying no also deletes the number if one was already stored.

What gets recorded when you open a page

Whether or not you agree to the number above, opening a page records:

  • which page or game it was, and when
  • the site that sent you here, if any — only the site, not the full address
  • whether the screen is phone, tablet or desktop sized

If you agreed, the random number is attached, which is what makes “12 visitors” mean twelve people rather than twelve page loads. If you didn’t answer, no number is attached and that view can never be linked to any other. If you said no, nothing is recorded at all.

Deliberately not recorded: your IP address, your exact location, your name unless you sign in and tell me, and anything at all about what you do on any other website. There is no fingerprinting.

If you sign in

Signing in is optional and the site works fully without it. It exists so your name can sit on your seat in the games instead of “Seat 2”.

Signing in uses Google. Google tells this site three things: your name, your email address, and your profile picture. That is all it sends and all that is kept. Google will know that you signed in to this site — their handling of that is covered by Google’s privacy policy, not this one.

Because the random number stays the same across signing in, your earlier visits from this browser get connected to your name at that point. If you would rather they didn’t, decline the number above before you sign in, or clear your browser storage first.

Where it lives and how long

In a Supabase database hosted in Tokyo, owned by me and read by nobody else. The visit log is readable by exactly one account — mine. That isn’t a promise about how careful I am; the database itself refuses the read for every other account, including anyone who digs the public key out of this page’s source.

Visits older than 180 days are deleted automatically, every night.

Getting rid of it

  • Stop the counting — say no above. That is immediate, and deletes the stored number.
  • Clear everything this site has put on your device — clear site data for this domain in your browser settings. Nothing here survives that.
  • Delete your account and everything attached to it — message me on Instagram and I’ll do it. There is no email address on this site on purpose: an address that drops mail is worse than no address.

Children

This site isn’t aimed at children and doesn’t knowingly keep anything about anyone under 13. If that has happened, tell me and it goes.

Changes

If what is collected ever changes, this page changes with it and the date at the top moves. If a change means collecting something genuinely new, the bar at the bottom will ask again rather than assume the old answer still covers it.
